LLM measurement

My LLM research focuses on measuring LLM behavior, correcting LLM-based measurements and treatment-effect estimates, and allocating human data collection.

I examine model behavior through its outputs, internal representations, and sensitivity to prompts. HuMCal calibrates synthetic responses against human benchmarks, and LLM Measurement corrects treatment-effect estimates based on LLM-scored outcomes. PRISM studies where to collect human data when the budget cannot cover every question or group.

SUVA

An external perspective on AI behavior.

When an organization delegates choices to an AI agent, it needs to understand how that agent behaves across situations. SUVA structures an audit around the situation, its stated understanding, the priorities expressed in its reasoning, and its action.

The published study examines social preferences across eight models using behavioral games and evaluates how alignment changes observed behavior. Stated reasoning is treated as an observable output.

Mental Accounting

An internal perspective on behavioral biases.

People often value money differently depending on its source or intended use. Models can reproduce these patterns in their answers. This research studies the internal representations that contribute to those choices and tests their role by intervening on them.

In the tested large models, targeted internal interventions shift specified economic choices. The results connect behavioral observation to mechanistic evidence, with limits that vary across models.

When Prompts Matter

Making the measurement specification explicit.

An AI-generated score depends on how the question, construct, and scoring rules are specified. This work documents that instrument and examines how estimates vary across a defined family of prompts.

The current study combines a sentiment benchmark, a retrospective application, and statistical analysis. Its aim is to make sensitivity and uncertainty interpretable for the measurement design actually used.

HuMCal

Correcting synthetic measurements.

Different AI prompts, personas, and model versions can produce different pictures of a customer population. HuMCal learns how to combine their responses using aggregate human benchmarks.

Evaluated with transaction-based market structure and public-opinion data, the method improves agreement on calibration data and in most tested transfers. Performance in a new population or setting requires validation.

Working research

LLM Measurement

Correcting treatment-effect estimates.

A new service experience may change both what customers say and how an AI scorer responds to their language. An apparent improvement can therefore combine a real effect with a scoring difference.

This research combines full-sample AI scores with a probability sample of human judgments to estimate the effect on the human-defined outcome.

Evidence includes statistical theory, corpus backtests, and retrospective audits of an archived randomized experiment. The human-audit design and its assumptions determine what the corrected estimate supports.

PRISM

Informing the allocation of human data collection.

Synthetic responses may look convincing overall while disagreeing with people on particular questions, brands, or customer groups. PRISM studies how to identify those risks and use them to inform where additional human measurement would be most useful.

The practical focus is selective data collection when a limited budget cannot cover every setting. The value of that allocation depends on the measurement task and available resources.
